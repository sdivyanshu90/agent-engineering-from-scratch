# Mastery gates

For each day, pass public tests, two independently chosen edge cases, at least
4/5 exam points including independent implementation, and an unseen trace.
Record one misconception corrected and a delayed review date. Reference answers
are [separate](solutions/examinations/week-01.md).

Week 1 requires 80/100 on the [weekly exam](assessments/weekly/week-01.md), including
valid success JSON, nonzero operational-failure status, visible decode failures,
and fresh per-call state. These critical criteria cannot be compensated by a
high conceptual score. Retry with a new fixture after remediation.

Planned phase gates: foundations (CLI/API/math reconstruction); ML/LLMs (gradient
and attention correctness); applications (typed outputs and retrieval benchmark);
agents (budgeted durable execution); reliability (interoperability and adversarial
regressions); production (recovery and technical defense). Weeks 1–2 and Phase 1 gates are currently authored. See [assessment status](assessments/phase-examinations/README.md).
Do not mark planned gates passed from a topic list.

A diagnostic can replace instruction only if it demonstrates the same evidence.
A vague claim of prior Python experience cannot waive the implementation or
failure analysis. Gates are fixed before grading; record errors and remediate
rather than lowering criteria to obtain a pass.

Week 2 requires [its weekly gate](assessments/weekly/week-02.md) and the
[Phase 1 cumulative gate](assessments/phase-examinations/phase-01.md).

Weeks 3–4 additionally require [Week 3](assessments/weekly/week-03.md),
[Week 4](assessments/weekly/week-04.md) and the
[Phase 2 cumulative defense](assessments/phase-examinations/phase-02.md).

Weeks5–6 require their daily/weekly contracts and the
[Phase3 grounded-application defense](assessments/phase-examinations/phase-03.md).
Quote validity, useful answers and authorization must be defended separately.
